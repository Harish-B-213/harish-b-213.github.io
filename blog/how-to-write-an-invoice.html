How to write an invoice (free template)

Updated 2 September 2026 · 6 minute read

An invoice tells your customer what they owe, by when, and how to pay. Get it right and most customers pay on time without being chased. Get it wrong and the invoice sits in someone's inbox while they wait for you to send a corrected one.

What a UK invoice must include

  • A unique invoice number, in sequence
  • Your business name, address and contact details
  • Your customer's name and address
  • The date of the invoice and the date the work was done
  • A description of each item, with the price
  • The total, and the VAT if you are VAT registered, with your VAT number
  • Your payment terms and how to pay

Step by step

Start with the invoice number and date. Add your customer's details exactly as they appear on their paperwork, because finance teams match invoices to purchase orders by name. List each piece of work on its own line with a clear description and a price. Add VAT on each line if you charge it, then the total. Finish with the due date and your bank details or a payment link.

Payment terms that get you paid

Thirty days is common, but shorter terms are paid faster. Fourteen days works well for freelancers and trades. Put the due date as a real date, such as 30 September, not "net 30", because a date is easier to act on.
