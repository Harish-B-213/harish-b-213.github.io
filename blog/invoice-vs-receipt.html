Invoice vs receipt: what is the difference?

Updated 18 June 2026 · 4 minute read

An invoice asks for payment. A receipt confirms that payment was made. You send an invoice before you are paid and a receipt afterwards, and many small businesses only ever need the first, because a paid invoice does the job of a receipt.

When you need an invoice

Send an invoice whenever a customer will pay you later: after a job, at the end of a month, or for a deposit. It sets out what they owe and by when, and it is the document your customer's finance team needs to release the money.

When you need a receipt

A receipt is useful when a customer pays on the spot, in cash or by card, and wants proof for their own records or expenses. It shows the date, the amount and what it was for.

Can one document do both?

Yes. Once an invoice is paid, mark it as paid and send it again. Tidewater does this automatically, and adds the payment date and method, so your customer has both in one place.
